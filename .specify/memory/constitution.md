# ToDo App Constitution

## Core Principles

### I. Family-centered usefulness
The product should serve a family coordinating everyday tasks. Decisions should be grounded in the needs of the people who will use it together; do not assume a particular family structure, age range, or household routine without confirmation.

### II. Simplicity first
The app should make it straightforward to keep track of family tasks. Avoid unnecessary complexity and favor understandable concepts and interactions. Specific task workflows remain for feature specifications.

### III. Priority and focus
Helping users stay focused on what matters most is a stated product priority. Keep this goal visible when making product decisions; the particular prioritization model is not yet decided.

### IV. Usable and inclusive experience
Design for family members with differing levels of technical confidence and accessibility needs. Keep language clear and interactions learnable. Detailed usability and accessibility acceptance criteria belong in feature planning.

### V. Quality through verification
Changes should be verifiable with tests appropriate to their risk and behavior. Feature plans should identify acceptance criteria and meaningful automated or manual checks before implementation. No particular test framework or workflow has been selected.

## Constraints and Open Decisions

- Confirmed product context: a simple ToDo app for adding tasks for the user's family, with staying focused on priorities as the most important initial product outcome.
- No technology stack, target platform, data-sharing model, privacy/security policy, or deployment constraint has been specified. Feature plans must make relevant decisions explicit rather than assume them.
- Working assumption for this proposal: family task information should be treated as personal data and handled with appropriate care. Specific access, retention, and security requirements require owner review and feature-level decisions.

## Development and Review

- Each feature should have a clear user problem, scope, and testable acceptance criteria before implementation.
- Reviewers should check that proposed changes serve the stated family-centered simplicity and priority-focus principles.
- Keep implementation choices proportionate to demonstrated needs; record material trade-offs for review.

## Governance

This constitution is a proposed project-wide baseline. It does not override the need for explicit owner review and approval in the workspace. Amendments should state what changed and why, and dependent feature documents should be reviewed when a principle or constraint changes. Unresolved product choices must remain explicit; a draft or assumption is not an approved decision.
